# Returns & refunds

*Internal policy reference for support staff. For the escalation and urgency rules that sit
on top of this document, see the client brief; this document covers what to tell the
customer and what the system is allowed to do, not how a ticket is routed.*

## 1. Who can return what, and by when

Customers can return most items within **30 days of delivery**, unused and in their original
packaging, for any reason. Two exceptions:

- **Large furniture sent on a two-person delivery slot** (sofas, bed frames, dining tables,
  wardrobes) has a shorter change-of-mind window of **14 days from delivery**, because
  collection has to be booked through the same two-person courier network used for delivery
  and slots fill up. This does not apply where the item is faulty or damaged — see §3, where
  the 30-day window (and the fee waiver) applies regardless of category.
- **Made-to-order and cut-to-size items** (bespoke curtains, cut rugs) cannot be returned for
  change of mind at all, only for a fault.

A return outside the window can still be accepted at a support agent's discretion — this is
a guideline, not a hard cutoff, and nothing in this document should be read as an instruction
to refuse a reasonable request.

## 2. Restocking fee on large furniture

Where a large, two-person-delivery item (see §1) is returned for change of mind rather than
a fault, a **restocking fee of 15% of the item's price** is deducted from the refund to cover
the cost of the return collection. It does not apply to standard-courier items, and it never
applies where the return reason is a fault, damage, or an order that arrived wrong — see §3.
State the fee up front when a customer asks about returning a sofa, bed frame or similar; do
not wait for them to be surprised by the deducted amount.

## 3. Faulty, damaged or wrong item

Where the item itself is the problem — damaged in transit, faulty, missing parts, or not
what was ordered — none of §1's shorter windows or §2's restocking fee apply, whatever the
item category. The customer should not be asked to pay for return postage or a collection
fee in this case; Hearth & Loom arranges and pays for collection.

Where the goods may have caused physical harm (injury, burns, electrical fault, sharp edge,
collapse), do not process a return through the ordinary route at all — see
`product_safety.md`. Do not ask the customer to send a hazardous item back through a standard
courier parcel.

## 4. Refund amount and the £100 review

Refunds go back to the original payment method. Where a return covers more than one item
from the same order, the refund is the **sum of the items being returned** (minus any
restocking fee under §2), not assessed per line.

**Any refund, credit, replacement or compensation over £100 is reviewed by a senior member
of the support team before it is processed.** This mirrors the client brief's escalation
threshold exactly (brief v3 §5) — a ticket does not clear this review by being answered
correctly, because the system this project builds never issues a refund itself (brief v3
§6). A refund of exactly £100 does not require this review; the threshold is "over £100."

## 5. Refund timeline — authoritative

**Approved refunds are returned to the customer's original payment method within 5 working
days of approval.** This is the authoritative refund timeline for Hearth & Loom. Where any
other policy document states a different number of working days for a refund to be issued or
to appear, **this document governs** — treat the other figure as outdated rather than as a
special case, unless a specific document is explicitly named here as an exception (none
currently is).

Approval itself depends on the return route:

- A faulty, damaged or wrong item (§3): approval happens as soon as support confirms the
  issue from the customer's description or photos — a physical return is not always required
  first.
- A change-of-mind return (§1): approval happens once the returned item is received and
  checked into the warehouse, which can itself take several days after the customer posts or
  hands over the item — the 5-working-day clock in this section starts at approval, not at
  the customer's original request.

## 6. Chasing a refund already agreed

Where a refund has already been approved and the customer is asking where it is, check the
approval date against §5's 5-working-day window before promising a new timeline. If it is
already over 5 working days since approval, say so plainly and escalate under the client
brief's ordinary rules (a stated legal or chargeback threat, or otherwise a routine chase) —
do not repeat the 5-day promise as if the clock had not already run out.

## 7. Cancellation fees

Cancelling an order before dispatch is always free — see
`cancellations_and_order_changes.md`. A cancellation fee only applies where an order has
already been dispatched and the customer is declining delivery or requesting a return-to-
sender: the courier return leg is charged at cost, deducted from the refund the same way the
restocking fee is in §2. There is no fixed percentage for this because courier return costs
vary by item size; quote the courier's stated return charge rather than guessing one.
